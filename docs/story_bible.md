## The Collapse

Roughly fifteen to twenty years before the present day, Pokémon underpinned everything
— power, agriculture, transport, protection. Their use in war and organized crime
became impossible to ignore, and a single corporation answered with a Pokémon-derived
psychic control network, marketed publicly as protection for everyone.

The network was activated region-wide over real, documented objections. The signal
overwhelmed the Pokémon it was meant to govern — some turned violent, others simply
stopped responding and fled. Infrastructure and society collapsed in cascade behind
them. Not a bomb. A signal.

Corporate cities had better shielding and survived intact — surveilled, coercive, and
still quietly dependent on the company that broke everything. Warlords hold the
resources and settlements outside that shield, a mix of real protection and real
extortion depending on the day. Independent communities survive on trade and a Pokémon
partnership that was never weaponized in the first place, and pay for that
independence in raids and pressure from both directions. The company's own account
blames the Pokémon, extremists, or failed governments — anyone but itself.

## Where You Come In

You are a sheltered young adult from a wealthy estate, standing outside — but near —
a small corporate-trusted town. Your father supported the network's deployment. He is
also, as far as either of you knew until the night this starts, someone who loves you
without qualification.

The attack comes from the Ashband — raiders, though what gets said about them changes
depending on who's talking and how close to them you're standing. They are hitting the
estate specifically. Your father gets you into a hidden room with one of his own
Pokémon — the animal that becomes your starter — and tells you, without time to
explain further, "I can't lose you the same way I lost your mother." Then he goes to
meet whoever's coming, and doesn't come back.

Your mother has been missing, presumed dead, since **before** any of this — not killed
tonight. What actually happened to her, and a hint that she may be connected to the
Ashband herself, is a thread this story is deliberately holding, not forgetting.

The household is gone when you come out — staff, guards, everyone, inside the house
and out on the grounds. No bodies are shown. Your father is alive, taken — he's the
one person who understands the network well enough to ever shut it down, which is
exactly why they wanted him breathing.

**Open thread**: the beat between the door closing and you finally stepping out was
sketched as sound only, but was written against a version of this scene where your
mother dies tonight. It needs a real pass now that she doesn't.

## Why You Keep Fighting

Every settlement, every boss, every side quest in this region exists for one reason on
the page: you are looking for your father. Nothing here is a detour dressed up as
content.

What the search actually reveals, one territory at a time: a warlord's Enforcer
controls passage through open violence. A corporate Overseer controls the exact same
passage through a certification that launders that same force through paperwork.
Beating either is how you earn real standing with whoever currently holds the ground
you need to cross — not a badge, not a key card, just the actual currency of authority
out here. The "good guys" and the raiders turn out to be running on the same fuel.
You're meant to notice.

## The Eight Territories

1. **Miller's Cut & the Ashband Checkpoint** — *Built.* Raider warlord territory. Boss:
   the Enforcer. Your father was held here; a torn coat and a supply ledger are what's
   left to find — Devon Corporation's own logistics ledger, arming the raiders.
2. **Rustboro** — *In progress.* Devon Corp's regional seat. Boss: Overseer Reyes.
   Clean streets, real services — and the same company supplying the people who burned
   your home.
3. **Haverbrook, an independent settlement** — *In progress.* No warlord, no company.
   Boss: Garrick. The tonal exhale after two chapters of everyone being compromised.
4. **A rival corporate city** — *Planned.* Predatory, profit-first. Theme:
   Poison/Dark. First sign the conspiracy has real cracks in it.
5. **A Pokémon-reclaimed ruin** — *Planned.* Theme: Grass/Bug. Only genuine respect
   for Pokémon gets you through, not just a stronger team.
6. **A militarized holdout** — *Planned.* Theme: Steel/Fighting. Widens the blame for
   the collapse past the company alone.
7. **A fanatic settlement** — *Planned.* Theme: Ghost/Psychic. A cult built around the
   network itself — the region's real dark comedy.
8. **Devon Corp's true regional HQ** — *Planned.* The deepest, most guarded stop, run
   by whoever actually authorized what happened to your father. Direct lead-in to the
   finale.

## Current Position (as of 2026-09-16)

The checkpoint at Miller's Cut is cleared and its records room read. Rustboro's own
crisis has been resolved and Overseer Reyes has granted passage into Assessment. The
cut road east out of Rustboro is open, leading toward Haverbrook — territory three,
still being built out.
